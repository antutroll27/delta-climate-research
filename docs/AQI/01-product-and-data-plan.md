# AQI Product and Data Plan

**Version:** 1.2  
**Date:** 26 September 2026  
**Status:** Kolkata scope approved; see the [first-release spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-aqi-kolkata-design.md)  
**Related:** [Documentation index](./README.md)

## 1. Product objective

OBOS should help a user answer four questions without overstating certainty:

1. What has a real government monitoring station recently measured?
2. Which CPCB AQI category does the available observation support?
3. How relevant is that instrument to the selected ward?
4. Where is there no credible live coverage yet?

The first release is a government-station measurement and coverage product. It is
not an air-quality forecast, a street-level pollution surface, a thermal-model
validation layer, or an OBOS-device deployment. First-party ESP32 measurements are
a later phase after the government feed is stable in production.

### User-facing outcome

For each OBOS area, the interface should provide:

- the latest valid CPCB AQI when the source data can support it;
- component pollutant concentrations and their units;
- the government station name and type;
- observation time and freshness;
- spatial relationship to the selected area;
- source, standard and calculation method;
- a visible data-quality or coverage state; and
- a short health-category description sourced from CPCB.

If the ward has no qualifying measurement, the interface should say so plainly and
may show the nearest station with its distance. A nearest station is contextual
evidence, not the ward's measured AQI.

## 2. Evidence classes

The API and UI must use these evidence classes consistently.

| Class | Meaning | Allowed presentation |
|---|---|---|
| `regulatory_station` | Measurement published by a regulatory or official monitoring network | Station marker, current value, history and CPCB-derived AQI |
| `obos_device` | Reading from a provisioned OBOS device with known calibration state | Device marker, reading, quality status and history |
| `modelled_estimate` | Value generated by an external atmospheric model | Separate contextual layer labelled **Modelled estimate** |
| `historical_archive` | Non-live archive retained for trends and research | Historical charts with its exact date range and limitations |
| `no_coverage` | No qualifying observation for the requested place/time | Explicit empty state; never rendered as zero or green |

Values from different classes may be compared, but they must not be silently merged
into one measured field.

## 3. Source strategy

### 3.1 Primary source: OpenAQ v3

OpenAQ is the preferred acquisition layer for live, measured station data during the
pilot because it exposes station coordinates, measurement timestamps, parameters,
units, providers and historical observations through a consistent API.

Implementation policy:

- call OpenAQ from the server-side TypeScript AQI function, never directly from
  the browser;
- carry the OpenAQ location ID, provider, owner and licence metadata in every
  normalized result and any future archive;
- retain the upstream observation timestamp separately from the fetch timestamp;
- discover stations from current coordinates rather than a permanent hard-coded
  station count;
- cache normal reads for 10–15 minutes;
- treat provider limits as configuration and re-check them before launch;
- **build IST clock-hour means from OpenAQ's raw readings, never from its `/hours`
  endpoint.** On 26 September 2026, OpenAQ's raw 15-minute values for Ballygunge
  matched the OpenCity archive reading for reading, but OpenAQ omits one quarter-hour
  in four (the one ending at IST :45), and its UTC hours straddle IST hours. Its own
  hourly means therefore differ from the full data by about 6 µg/m³ per hour; and
- **select sensors by unit, not by parameter name.** CO, NO₂ and SO₂ each appear as
  two sensors (ppb and µg/m³); CPCB breakpoints need µg/m³ (mg/m³ for CO).

The research snapshot on 25 September 2026 showed a free allowance of 60 requests
per minute and 2,000 per hour. This is sufficient for a cached pilot, but the
current [OpenAQ documentation](https://docs.openaq.org/) remains authoritative.

### 3.2 Official fallback: CPCB through data.gov.in

The CPCB/data.gov.in resource has a commercially suitable GODL-India route and may
serve as an official fallback or cross-check. The currently advertised fields
include location and pollutant summaries, but the researched schema did not make
the observation timestamp or averaging semantics sufficiently clear for it to be
the preferred computation input.

Before adopting it for live calculations, verify:

- the timestamp attached to each record;
- whether `min`, `max` and `avg` describe an hour or another period;
- unit consistency by pollutant;
- pagination and late-update behaviour; and
- whether the deployed response matches the catalogue schema.

Commercial use must continue to follow the
[Government Open Data License — India](https://data.gov.in/government-open-data-license-india).

### 3.3 Historical source: WBPCB archives through OpenCity

The repository contains seven Kolkata station archives and a derived daily file.
They are suitable for historical co-exposure and seasonality work. The builder
preserves daily coverage counts and flags sparse days.

OpenCity's detailed Ballygunge resource ends on 2025-12-31 and stamps each 15-minute
reading at its IST end time. Its units need checking column by column: the pressure
column is labelled "BP (mmHg)" but holds values near 1,010, which can only be hPa.

The catalogue does not unambiguously state whether every published value is a
concentration or an already-derived index. Consequently, this archive must retain
its existing conservative unit description and must not be relabelled as CPCB AQI
without source confirmation.

### 3.4 Sources not selected for the measured production layer

| Source | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| WAQI / aqicn | Do not use in the commercial production path without a signed agreement | Published API terms restrict commercial use and redistribution; a shared token does not change the licence |
| Google Air Quality API | Optional later modelled-context layer | Useful high-resolution estimates and CPCB index support, but modelled values must stay distinct; terms, attribution and MapLibre compatibility require legal review |
| Open-Meteo hosted free tier | Do not use for production AQI | Hosted free service is non-commercial even where underlying data is openly licensed |
| IQAir | Not selected for the pilot | Adds cost and may re-serve the same regulatory network; confirm incremental coverage before purchase |
| Copernicus CAMS | Regional context only | Resolution is not suitable for ward-scale measured claims |

The previously supplied WAQI feed ID `A567541` resolves to Sarsuna College rather
than Ballygunge. It must not be mapped to Ballygunge. Because its token was shared
in a URL, it should be rotated even if WAQI remains limited to private experiments.
See the [WAQI API terms](https://aqicn.org/api/terms/).

## 5. Ward-to-station mapping policy

### 5.1 Selection order

1. Select a valid station or OBOS device whose coordinate falls inside the actual
   OBOS area boundary.
2. If multiple instruments qualify, return all of them and identify the selected
   representative using a documented completeness/freshness rule.
3. If no instrument is inside the boundary, find the nearest valid instrument and
   return it as `nearby`, including its geodesic distance.
4. If the nearest instrument exceeds the configured contextual radius or is stale,
   return `no_station` or `stale` instead of an AQI for the area.

**Decided 26 September 2026:** the boundary is each area's **3 km window**, the square
of side 3,000 m on the OBOS centre defined in `scripts/_types.py` `ward_bounds` and
exported in the heat-history vector file. Both Kolkata stations lay inside it on that
date; neither lay inside the 1.4 km box. Since 3 October Ballygunge's monitor lies 53 m
outside its window, because the window now centres on KMC Ward 68 (AQI-R20); it is kept
as the nearest official monitor and labelled `outside_window`. The response must state which geometry version was used,
and the UI always states the station's distance from the centre.

### 5.2 Coverage states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `inside_area` | Instrument point is within the selected boundary |
| `nearby` | Instrument is outside the boundary and shown only for context |
| `insufficient_data` | Instrument exists but the CPCB validity rules are not satisfied |
| `stale` | Latest observation exceeds the freshness threshold |
| `no_station` | No acceptable continuous instrument was found |
| `source_unavailable` | Upstream failed and no acceptable cached observation exists |

### 5.3 Map claims

The first release may show instrument markers, timestamps, histories and distances.
It must not paint a ward polygon with one station's value or interpolate a pollution
surface between sparse stations. A later spatial model would be a separate product,
with validation, uncertainty and modelled-data labelling.

## 6. CPCB AQI calculation specification

### 6.1 Pollutants and windows

CPCB defines sub-indices for:

- PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, NH3 and Pb using 24-hour treatment; and
- CO and O3 using 8-hour treatment.

The official report and calculator workbook are the numerical authorities:

- [CPCB National AQI](https://cpcb.nic.in/National-Air-Quality-Index/)
- [CPCB AQI final report](https://app.cpcbccr.com/ccr_docs/FINAL-REPORT_AQI_.pdf)
- [CPCB AQI calculator workbook](https://app.cpcbccr.com/ccr_docs/AQI-Calculator.xls)

The earlier repo design described CO and O3 as an “8-hour rolling maximum,” while
the official explanatory wording refers to an 8-hour average. Before coding, the
team must reproduce the workbook's exact behaviour and record whether a maximum of
rolling 8-hour means is required. This is an implementation gate, not a wording
choice.

### 6.2 Sub-index equation

For a concentration `C` between concentration breakpoints `B_LO` and `B_HI`, with
AQI breakpoints `I_LO` and `I_HI`:

```text
I = ((I_HI - I_LO) / (B_HI - B_LO)) × (C - B_LO) + I_LO
```

The overall AQI is the maximum valid pollutant sub-index. It is not a mean of the
available sub-indices.

### 6.3 Publication validity

A CPCB AQI may be published only when all required validity tests pass:

- at least three pollutants have valid sub-indices;
- at least one is PM2.5 or PM10; and
- a pollutant sub-index has at least 16 hours of observations in the applicable
  calculation period, subject to confirmation against the official workbook.

Missing readings remain missing. They must not be converted to zero, forward-filled
silently or filled by a model inside the measured series.

### 6.4 Calculation output

Persist and return:

- the raw observations used;
- averaging-window start and end;
- observation count and expected count;
- computed mean or prescribed statistic;
- pollutant sub-index;
- overall AQI and dominant pollutant;
- CPCB category;
- algorithm version and breakpoint-table version; and
- invalidity reasons when no AQI can be published.

## 7. Quality and claims policy

- `observed_at` describes the measurement; `received_at` describes arrival at OBOS;
  `computed_at` describes the derived AQI.
- Units are normalized only through explicit parameter mappings. The original unit
  and value are retained.
- Regulatory, OBOS-device and modelled readings remain separate series.
- Device readings carry calibration status, firmware version and sensor identity.
- No AQI value is described as “ward-wide,” “street-level” or “real-time” unless the
  data and latency support that exact claim.
- “Live” means a periodically refreshed feed and must be accompanied by the actual
  observation time.
- AQI is contextual co-exposure information and does not validate or alter the heat
  simulation.
- ML/RL training is blocked until the dataset has documented calibration, drift,
  missingness, spatial sampling bias, permitted use and a held-out evaluation plan.
